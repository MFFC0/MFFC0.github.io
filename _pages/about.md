I am a research engineer working at the intersection of spintronics, quantum hardware, and neuromorphic computing. My research explores how nanoscale magnetic devices can enable energy-efficient computation and precise control of quantum systems.

My doctoral research at Virginia Commonwealth University focused on nanomagnet-based reservoir computing and quantum control. Through numerical modeling and experimental studies, I investigated how magnetic devices can process information and generate localized fields for controlling spin qubits. My work spans three main areas:

- Coherent control of nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond using acoustically driven nanomagnets.
- Localized quantum control using nanomagnets and magnetic skyrmions.
- Physical reservoir computing with nanomagnetic systems for classification and time-series prediction.

A central contribution of my research is the experimental demonstration of coherent quantum control using a nanoscale magnet driven by surface acoustic waves. This work explores a path toward more energy-efficient and spatially selective control for quantum computing and sensing.

## Research interests

- Spintronics and magnetic device physics
- Quantum control and sensing with nitrogen-vacancy centers
- Thin-film process development (PECVD, ALD) and dielectric gap-fill
- Superconducting qubit calibration and benchmarking
- Emerging nonvolatile memory (MRAM) and neuromorphic computing
